# Inspire

Dig up ideas and material for something the user is thinking about or about to write: what they already thought, what they read, and links they haven't made yet. This gathers; it doesn't write the piece. For the writing itself, the `hylo-writing` skill takes it from here.

**Gather to completion, then answer once.**

---

## Step 1 — Get the seed

A topic, a question, a half-formed idea, or a draft. From a draft, take its main claims and the gaps it leaves. If the seed is just "give me something to write about", read `/_memory/_goals.md` and `/_memory/_state.md` and recent short notes (`short_list {"latest": 14}`) for what's on their mind, and start there.

---

## Step 2 — Gather from four places

1. **Their own thinking**: short notes, journal entries and `own` units on the seed. Half-finished thoughts and open questions count; they are often the best starting points.
2. **What they read**: quotes, examples, cases and data from `external` units and their source notes. Keep the original wording for a quote, and note who said it.
3. **Across domains**: the same idea in another area of their knowledge. Search the seed's core mechanism or tension, not its topic words, and look in the indexes of other domains.
4. **Old and forgotten**: search without a date filter, and prefer what hasn't been touched for a long time.

Search with several phrasings, as in finding. Read only the parts you'll use.

---

## Step 3 — Answer

Group the material, one item per line with `[[link]]` and date, and a few words on why it's useful for the seed. Omit empty groups. 8–15 items in all; quality over count.

```markdown
# Material for <seed>

## Your own thinking
- <the idea, in their words where possible> — [[note]], YYYY-MM-DD

## From what you read
- "<quote or example>" — <who> in [[note]]

## From elsewhere in your notes
- <the link to another area, and the angle it opens> — [[unit]]

## Questions you left open
- <a question still unanswered in their notes> — [[note]]
```

Then two or three angles the material suggests, a line each. End with `choices`, such as: save this as a material note, start writing with it, look further afield.
